Dense packings from Γ72, and the search for the next Leech lattice

Gabriele Nebe's 72-dimensional lattice Γ72 is the densest known packing in its dimension. Slicing it and restacking shifted copies gives new packings in dimensions 67 to 71. The same toolkit, pointed at dimension 96, closes several routes to an extremal lattice there and narrows down where one could still hide.

Five dimensions past the ceiling

Cut Γ72 along a small sublattice K of rank k. What remains is a lattice in dimension 72 − k. The antipode construction of Conway and Sloane stacks several shifted copies of that slice, one for each point of a tight cluster S in the dual of K. The copies sit slightly closer than in Γ72, but there are more of them:

δ = |S| · det(K)−1/2 · ((8 − β)/4)(72 − k)/2

Here β is the squared diameter of the cluster. The chart compares each packing with the best density that any slice of Γ72 could reach, a hard limit from the Hermite bound.

Each certificate is checked in exact arithmetic: the slice is saturated, every shifted copy is realized by an explicit integer vector of Γ72, and β and δ are computed as rationals. The results rely on Nebe's theorem that Γ72 has minimum 8. The standard tables list no packings at all in dimensions 65–71, so the fair comparison is with slices of Γ72; stacking layers on Nebe's 64-dimensional lattice has not been evaluated and could compete in dimension 67.

The search in dimension 96

An extremal even unimodular lattice in dimension 96 would have minimum norm 10, the next member of the family that starts with Leech in 24 and Γ72 in 72. Nobody knows whether one exists; dimension 88 was settled only in September 2026. These are the routes tried here.

  • Closed by proof
    Leech ⊗ a rank-4 partner over Q(√−23) The field has class number 3. Some ideal twist of Leech has roots, and pairing it with the twisted partner always produces a vector of norm at most 8. All 24 candidates built carry explicit certificates.
  • Closed by proof
    The same over Q(√−d) for d ≤ 8, including Γ72's ring The Hermite bound caps the partner's minimum at √d, so split vectors have norm at most 8.
  • Closed by proof
    Symmetric Paley codes, A = mC + cI over Z/N All 53 lattices die, including 14 that lattice reduction could not crack: a symmetry of order 47 leaves a 4-dimensional fixed sublattice that must contain a vector of norm at most 8.
  • Open
    A deformed Z/16 Paley code Even unimodular, verified exactly. Reduction up to block size 45 finds nothing shorter than norm 10, and one full layer of vectors is proven clear. The rest is not yet certified.
  • Lead
    E₈ ⊗ J₂₄ over Z[(1+√−7)/2] Class number 1, so no twist trap, and split vectors land exactly on norm 10. It needs an extremal 24-dimensional 7-modular lattice J, whose forced theta series 1 + 84672 q⁵ + 91728 q⁶ + … is admissible. None found yet: Leech is now built as Pb ⊗ E₈ with an explicit √−7, but its 7-modular sublattices, symmetric versions and Kneser neighbours all stall near 1,700 short vector pairs, and a complete search of ideal lattices in Q(ζ₃₅), Q(ζ₅₆) and Q(ζ₈₄) reaches minimum 8 at best.

Symmetries an extremal 96-dimensional lattice cannot have

A standard lemma on prime-order automorphisms, combined with Hermite and Blichfeldt bounds, gives a fast filter for any construction:

  • no automorphism of prime order 31 to 89, and in particular none of order 47;
  • order 97 only if it moves every vector, which makes the lattice an ideal lattice in Q(ζ₉₇);
  • order 23 only with a 74-dimensional fixed sublattice, and order 29 only with 68;
  • order 19 with fixed dimension 78 or 6, order 17 with 80 or none, order 13 with 84, 72, 12 or none.

What is and is not claimed

Proven or exactly certified: the packings in dimensions 66–71 (given Γ72's minimum), the obstructions for Q(√−23) and small discriminants, the explicit kills of all 24 Hermitian candidates and all 53 symmetric Paley lattices, the mod-4 layer of the Z/16 candidate, and the automorphism filter.

Computed under GRH: the complete ideal-lattice search for J in Q(ζ₃₅), Q(ζ₅₆) and Q(ζ₈₄), which relies on PARI's class groups and units.

Evidence only: lattice-reduction searches that find no short vector, and the genus walk over Q(√−11).

Not claimed: record status in dimensions 65–71, and anything about whether an extremal 96-dimensional lattice exists.
